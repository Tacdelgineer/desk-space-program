Opening the case

Your GPU isn't slow.It's starving.

Inside a DGX Spark, the model lives in the memory chips. To write each token, the whole model crosses the memory bus to the GPU. The GPU does its math in a flash, then waits for the next delivery.

What's real on this page

  • The specs are published figures: 128 GB of shared memory, 273 GB/s of memory bandwidth, a 20-core Arm CPU next to a Blackwell GPU, and a 4 TB SSD.
  • The board is an illustration, not a teardown. Chip count, placement and the cooler are simplified. Each glowing cell on a memory chip is 1 GB.
  • Speeds are estimates from simple rules, not measurements. Writing speed is bandwidth divided by the bytes read per token. Reading speed is compute divided by model size, and the compute figures are rough placeholders.
  • The system keeps about 8 GB for itself, shown as the dim cells.
  • GPU math used is the share of the GPU's math capacity in use. nvidia-smi shows a much higher busy figure while writing, because it counts any moment a program is running on the GPU, even one that mostly waits for memory.
  • Not modeled yet: many requests at once, heat, and the extra work of attention on very long prompts.

In the real series the Spark's numbers are measured on the machine. Other machines stay marked as estimated until they're measured.
